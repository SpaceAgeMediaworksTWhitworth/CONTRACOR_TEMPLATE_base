Filing a roof claim can feel like a lot. There are forms, phone calls, an adjuster visit, and an estimate full of line items. {{business}} helps homeowners in {{city}} with the roofing side of it so you can focus on the rest.

Here's the short version. Your claim is between you and your insurance company. We're the roofer. We find and document the damage, meet the adjuster, and do the work. We don't negotiate your claim for you and we don't act as your adjuster.

## When a claim makes sense

Most homeowners policies cover sudden damage, like hail or wind. They usually don't cover a roof that is just old and worn out. A claim may make sense if:

- A storm came through and left damage
- Damage is spread across the roof, not just one shingle
- The repair cost is more than your deductible

If the damage is small, a simple repair may cost less than your deductible. We'll tell you what we see so you can decide.

## Step 1: Get the roof checked

Before you call your insurance company, it helps to know what's there. We'll get on the roof and check shingles, flashing, vents, gutters, and siding. We take clear photos and write down what we find. You get a copy.

## Step 2: You file the claim

If there's real damage, you call your insurance company or agent to open a claim. Have these ready:

- The date of the storm
- Photos of the damage
- Photos of any water inside
- Your policy number

Check your policy for time limits. Many policies want storm damage reported within a certain time.

## Step 3: The adjuster visit

Your insurance company will send an adjuster to look at the roof. With your OK, we can be there too. We'll walk the roof with them and point out what we found. This helps make sure nothing gets missed. The adjuster works for the insurance company and makes their own call.

## Step 4: Review the estimate

After the visit, you get an estimate from your insurer. These can be hard to read. We'll sit down with you and explain what's on it in plain words. If something looks missing, we can share our photos and notes so you can ask your insurer about it.

## Step 5: The work gets done

Once you and your insurer settle things, we set a date and do the job. You pay your deductible. That's part of your policy, and it's the law. Any roofer who offers to skip or cover your deductible is putting you at risk.

## What affects the timeline

- How busy insurers are after a big storm
- How fast the adjuster can come out
- Whether extra review is needed
- Weather and material timing

We'll keep you posted on our part of the process.

## Things to watch out for

After a big storm, some roofers make big promises. Be careful with anyone who:

- Says they can "guarantee" your claim gets approved
- Offers to pay or waive your deductible
- Asks you to sign papers that let them handle your claim
- Wants a large payment up front

A good roofer helps you document the damage and does quality work. The claim itself stays between you and your insurance company.
