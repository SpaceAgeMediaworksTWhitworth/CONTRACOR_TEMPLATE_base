## What counts as a roof emergency

If water can get into your home, it's an emergency. Common ones we see:

- Wind rips off shingles and leaves the wood open
- A tree or big branch crashes into the roof
- Hail breaks through in spots
- A slow leak suddenly gets much worse
- Ice dams push water inside during a thaw

Water does damage fast. It soaks insulation, stains ceilings, warps floors, and can lead to mold. The sooner it's stopped, the less you'll have to fix later.

## What to do before we get there

Stay safe first. Then:

1. **Move your stuff.** Get furniture, rugs, and electronics away from the drip.
2. **Catch the water.** Use buckets, pots, and towels.
3. **Watch the power.** If water is near lights, fans, or outlets, turn off that area at the breaker.
4. **Take photos.** Get pictures of the damage inside and out, if you can do it safely.
5. **Stay off the roof.** Wet roofs are slick and storm damage can make them weak.

If a tree is on the house or power lines are down, stay clear and call the power company.

## How emergency tarping works

A tarp is a heavy cover we put over the damaged part of the roof. We fasten it so wind doesn't catch it and run it past the ridge so water can't slide under the top edge. Done right, it keeps water out until we can make the real repair.

We may also use a quick patch for small spots. It depends on the damage and the weather.

## Tarp vs the real repair

A tarp is a short-term fix. It's not meant to last for months. Sun and wind wear it down. Once the weather clears, we come back to:

- Replace missing or broken shingles
- Fix damaged wood
- Reseal flashing and vents
- Replace the roof if the damage is too big to patch

We'll show you photos and give you a written quote for the lasting fix.

## What affects the cost

- How big the damaged area is
- How steep and high the roof is
- Whether a tree or debris has to be cleared first
- The time of day and weather
- What materials the lasting repair needs

## Insurance and emergency repairs

Most policies expect you to take reasonable steps to stop more damage after a storm. That's what a tarp does. Keep your receipts and photos. Call your insurance company to ask what's covered. We can share our photos and notes with you to help. Your claim is between you and your insurer.
