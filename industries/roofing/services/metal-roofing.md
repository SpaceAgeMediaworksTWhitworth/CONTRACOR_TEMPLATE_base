## Is metal right for you?

Metal is a great fit if you:

- Plan to stay in your home a long time
- Want fewer repairs over the years
- Like the look
- Need a roof for a barn, shop, or garage

It may not be the best pick if you're selling soon or need the lowest upfront cost. Shingles cost less to start. Metal usually costs more at first but can last much longer.

## Types of metal roofing

**Standing seam.** The panels lock together with raised seams, and the screws are hidden. It has a clean look and fewer spots for water to get in. This is the most popular choice for homes.

**Exposed fastener.** The panels screw straight through into the roof. You'll see rows of screws with rubber washers. It costs less and is common on barns and shops. The washers wear out over time, so the screws need checking every so often.

**Metal shingles.** Metal made to look like shingles, slate, or wood shakes. A good pick if you want metal but a more classic look.

## Metal types and coatings

Most home metal roofs are steel with a protective coating. Aluminum is lighter and won't rust, but it can cost more. The paint finish matters too. Better finishes hold their color longer and resist fading and chalking in the sun.

## Metal roofs and our weather

Here in the Midwest, roofs deal with hail, wind, heavy snow, and big temperature swings. Metal handles a lot of this well:

- Snow and ice slide off more easily
- Panels hold up well in wind when put on right
- It doesn't crack from freeze and thaw like some materials can

A few things to plan for:

- Big hail can dent metal. Thicker panels help.
- Snow can slide off in big sheets. Snow guards over doors and walkways can help.
- Metal grows and shrinks with heat and cold. It has to be put on with that in mind, or screws and seams can loosen.
