A new roof is a big job. It's also one you should only have to do once or twice in the time you own your home. At {{business}}, we want you to know exactly what you're getting, what it costs, and why, before anyone climbs a ladder.

## When it's time for a new roof

Every roof wears out. Sun, wind, hail, and our freeze and thaw winters all take their toll. A regular shingle roof often lasts about 20 to 30 years, but storms and poor attic airflow can cut that short.

Here are the signs we look for:

- Shingles that curl, crack, or blow off
- Bald spots where the grit has worn away
- Grit piling up in your gutters
- Soft or saggy spots in the roof
- Water stains on ceilings or in the attic
- Many repairs in the last few years

One bad spot can usually be fixed. When the problems are all over the roof, a repair just buys a little time.

## Repair or replace?

We'll tell you straight. If a repair makes sense, we'll say so. If your roof is near the end of its life, we'll show you the photos and explain why patching it would cost you more over time. You make the call.

## Your material options

Most homes in the {{region}} use asphalt shingles. They come in a few types:

- **Three-tab shingles.** Flat and simple. Fewer homes use them now.
- **Architectural shingles.** Thicker, with more of a layered look. This is what most people pick.
- **Impact-rated shingles.** Built to stand up better to hail. Some insurance companies offer a discount for them, so it's worth asking your agent.

We also install metal roofing if you want something that lasts longer. See our metal roofing page for more.

A good roof is more than shingles. It also needs the right underlayment, flashing around chimneys and walls, drip edge, and proper vents. These parts are where a lot of roofs fail, so we don't skip them.

## What changes the price

We don't list prices here because every roof is different. These are the things that move the number:

- How big your roof is
- How steep it is
- How many old layers need to come off
- How much rotted wood needs to be replaced
- The shingles and parts you choose
- Chimneys, skylights, and other things we have to work around

Your written quote spells all of this out. If we find bad wood once the old roof is off, we'll show you photos and tell you the cost before we fix it.

## How long it takes

Most roofs take one to three days. The weather is the big wild card. If rain is coming, we won't leave your roof open. We'll keep you in the loop on the schedule.

## What to expect on install day

- We show up early and set tarps over your plants and yard.
- The old roof comes off. It's loud, and some dust may fall in the attic. Cover anything up there you care about.
- We check the wood and fix bad spots.
- New underlayment, flashing, vents, and shingles go on.
- We run a magnet over the yard and driveway for nails and haul off the old roof.
- We walk the finished job with you and answer questions.

Park your cars out of the driveway if you can. It keeps them safe and gives the crew room to work.

## How to pick a roofer

A new roof is a big spend, so take your time. Look for a local roofer with a real address and phone number. Ask for a written quote that lists the materials. Check their reviews. Ask who will be on your roof and who you call with questions. Be careful with anyone who shows up after a storm and pushes you to sign that day.
