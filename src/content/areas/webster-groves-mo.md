## Old homes, big trees

Webster Groves is known for its older homes and big, shady trees. Many streets are lined with Victorian, bungalow and Tudor style homes. Some go back more than 100 years.

These homes have a lot of character. They also have roofs with steep slopes, dormers and tall chimneys. That means more places where water can get in.

## What we find on older roofs

When we open up an older roof in Webster Groves, we often find:

- Two or three layers of old shingles
- Board decking instead of plywood
- Rusted or patched chimney flashing
- Worn valleys where two slopes meet
- Little or no attic venting

We tear off to the deck and fix what needs it. We show you photos and give you the cost for any extra work before we do it.

## Slate

Some older homes here still have slate. Slate can last a very long time, and broken pieces can often be swapped out. We will tell you honestly if yours can be repaired.

## Shade and moss

Big trees keep roofs shaded and damp. That leads to moss and dark streaks. Moss holds water against shingles and wears them out faster.

Leaves also clog gutters and valleys. When gutters overflow, water can rot the roof edge and fascia boards.

We check for moss and gutter problems on every inspection. We can clean, repair or replace gutters too.

## Historic homes

Some parts of Webster Groves may have historic rules for exterior work. If your home might be one of them, check with the city before you plan a new roof. We can help you find out.
