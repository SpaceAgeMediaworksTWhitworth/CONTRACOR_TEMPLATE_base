## Living among the trees

Weldon Spring is a quiet place with lots of trees. Many homes sit on bigger lots with woods close by. The Katy Trail and the Missouri River are right here too.

All those trees look great. But they are one of the hardest things on a roof.

## What trees do to roofs

Here is what we see on wooded lots:

- **Limb damage.** Wind and ice storms break limbs. A falling limb can crack shingles or punch a hole in the roof.
- **Scraping.** Branches that hang over the roof rub the shingles each time the wind blows.
- **Moss and dark streaks.** Shade keeps the roof damp. Moss grows and holds water on the shingles.
- **Clogged gutters.** Leaves pile up fast. Water backs up and can soak into the roof edge.

## Keeping your gutters working

In a place with this many trees, gutters need extra care. When they clog, water spills over or backs up under the shingles. That can rot the wood at the roof edge.

We can clean, repair or replace gutters. We can also talk with you about gutter guards.

## When a limb comes down

If a tree or limb hits your roof, call us. We can put a tarp on to keep rain out right away. Once the tree is cleared, we repair the damage.

If it happened in a storm, it may be covered by your insurance. We can take photos and help you with the claim.

## Bigger homes, bigger roofs

Many homes here have large roofs with lots of angles. Valleys collect leaves and water. We check these spots closely.
