## Living by the water

Lake Saint Louis is built around its lakes. That is a big part of why people love living here. But open water also means open wind.

Homes on the lake, and near it, take more wind than homes tucked between trees. Over time, that wind lifts shingle edges and loosens ridge caps. After a big storm, it is common to see shingles in the yard.

## Building a roof for wind

For homes with a lot of wind exposure, the details matter most. That means the right nails, in the right spots. It means good starter strips along the edges. It means ridge caps that are sealed down tight.

When we replace a roof here, we pay close attention to these steps. We also talk with you about shingles rated for higher wind.

## Community association approval

Much of Lake Saint Louis is under a community association. Many homes need approval before exterior work like a new roof. The rules can cover color and material.

We help with this part. We give you the exact product name, color and a sample if you need one. Then we wait for your approval before we start. That keeps you out of trouble with your neighbors and the association.

## Metal roofs

Some homeowners here like the look of metal roofing, and it holds up well to wind. It costs more up front but lasts a long time. If you are curious, ask us. We will tell you if it fits your home and your association rules.

## Bigger roofs, more angles

Many homes here have larger roofs with lots of valleys and angles. More valleys mean more places for water to collect. We check these areas closely on every inspection.
