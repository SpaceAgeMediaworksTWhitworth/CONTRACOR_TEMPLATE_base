## Roofs on older homes

Ballwin is full of solid homes built in the 1960s, 70s and 80s. Ranches, split levels and two-story homes line streets with big, mature trees.

Most of these homes have had at least one new roof by now. Some are ready for another. When we replace a roof on an older home, we do more than swap shingles.

## Tear off, then check the deck

Some older roofs have two layers of shingles. We take them all off, down to the wood. That lets us see the roof deck.

On homes this age, we sometimes find soft or rotted boards. That often happens near the edges, around chimneys, or where a leak was patched long ago. We replace the bad boards so the new roof has a solid base. We show you photos and tell you the cost first.

## Attic airflow

Many older homes were built with less attic venting than we use today. Without good airflow, heat and moisture get trapped. In summer, the attic bakes. In winter, damp air can cause frost and mold.

Both of these wear out a roof faster. When we replace a roof, we look at your intake and exhaust vents. If they need help, we will explain the fix in plain words.

## Big trees and gutters

Ballwin's mature trees are great for shade. But they drop leaves, seeds and limbs. Gutters clog fast. When they overflow, water can soak the roof edge and fascia boards.

We can clean, fix or replace gutters. We can also look at gutter guards if you are tired of cleaning them.

## Small repairs count too

Not every roof needs to be replaced. If your roof is in decent shape, a small fix can buy you years. Common repairs include new pipe boots, new flashing, and replacing a few storm-damaged shingles.
